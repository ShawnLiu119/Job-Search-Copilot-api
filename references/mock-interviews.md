# Role-specific mock interview questions

Create exactly five questions for every shortlisted role. Synthesize questions from evidence; do not copy a site's question list verbatim.

## Research order

Use current sources where available:

1. The captured job description and official job URL.
2. Official company pages describing products, strategy, values, culture, technical work, or the hiring process.
3. Public candidate-reported interview history from reputable career sites, professional communities, or first-person posts.
4. Role-family interview conventions only when company-specific history is unavailable.

Prefer primary sources for company facts. Record the URL and access date. Distinguish official statements from candidate reports. Do not bypass authentication, CAPTCHAs, robots restrictions, or paywalls.

## Five-question coverage

Choose five non-duplicative questions that cover these functions, adapting the form to the role:

1. **Core role expertise** - a required technical, analytical, operational, or domain competency from the JD.
2. **Applied case or problem solving** - a realistic company/product scenario requiring assumptions, tradeoffs, and a structured approach.
3. **Resume/project deep dive** - connects a central JD requirement to explicit candidate evidence without implying experience the resume does not show.
4. **Collaboration and values** - tests stakeholder management, communication, ambiguity, or a documented company value.
5. **Judgment and impact** - asks how the candidate would measure success, manage risk, prioritize, or learn from a failure in this role.

Historical evidence may justify changing the mix. For example, use more coding, statistics, SQL, system design, product sense, or case questions when multiple sources indicate that emphasis. Preserve five total questions.

## Output format

For each question include:

- **Question** - interview-ready wording.
- **Competency tested** - one concise label.
- **Why this role may ask it** - JD, company, or historical-interview evidence.
- **Strong answer should demonstrate** - three to five evaluation points, not a scripted answer.
- **Resume evidence hook** - a truthful experience, skill, or metric the candidate could draw on; use `gap` when none exists.
- **Likely follow-up** - one probing follow-up question.
- **Sources** - direct links labeled `official`, `job description`, or `candidate-reported`.

Start the file with company, role, job URL, preparation date, and this note:

> These are synthesized practice questions, not confirmed current interview questions. Candidate-reported history is directional and may be outdated.

## Quality gates

- All five questions must be materially specific to the company or role.
- At least three questions must cite a distinct JD requirement.
- At least one question must use official company evidence beyond the JD when available.
- Label every historical interview source as candidate-reported.
- Do not claim certainty about interview stages or questions without an official current source.
- Do not invent resume evidence, company facts, historical questions, or source links.
- Avoid generic prompts that could be used unchanged for any employer.
